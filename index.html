Research

I work in discrete geometry, where polyhedra, symmetry and incidence geometry meet. My dissertation, completed at Northeastern University in December 2025 under Egon Schulte, studies point-circle configurations in Euclidean 3-space that come from symmetric polyhedra and polytopes: the circles determined by a polyhedron's vertices and edges, the incidence structure they form, and the symmetries the configuration inherits from the polyhedron. For the regular skeletal polyhedra, the finite and infinite periodic polyhedra classified by Grünbaum and Dress, the configurations we obtain preserve every symmetry of the underlying polyhedron and are, in most cases, point-circle transitive.

The main tool is the E-construction, which builds a new configuration from a polytope out of vertices attached to chosen edges. It turns questions about which vertices of a regular skeletal polyhedron are coplanar into questions about incidence, and it extends from the polyhedron itself to its flag map. With Egon Schulte and [collaborator], a paper on conic configurations from symmetric polyhedra and polytopes is under review, and a third paper on [topic] is in preparation.

Three directions now interest me, each connecting these configurations to a neighbouring field. In discrete differential geometry, edge-tangent (Koebe) realizations of polyhedra carry circle patterns of their own, and I want to understand how they relate to the symmetric configurations above. In rigidity theory, the skeletal polyhedra are natural symmetric frameworks whose flexibility is governed by the same symmetry groups. And computationally, I am building tools in Python and SageMath to enumerate and visualize symmetric configurations, with geometric design and self-assembly in view.

Teaching

At Northeastern I was instructor of record for Mathematical Thinking (MATH 1215), Statistics and Software (MATH 2280) and Calculus for Business and Economics (MATH 1231, twice), and a teaching assistant for fifteen courses ranging from differential equations and linear algebra to real analysis, optimization, probability and statistics, and the applied mathematics capstone. I also taught in the Bridge to Calculus summer program.
